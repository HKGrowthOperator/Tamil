fix

1. **No real picture in the fold.** The stamp promises "photographs, the posts' own Commons pictures", but all four shots show none. A table on grey fills the top and the headline sits in text-only space. The table does the job of the picture, but there is no photograph of a festival, a temple or a cinema. The owner could supply a Commons photo from one of the 20 Rückblick posts. Placed beside the headline, it would give the "Deepavali ist vorbei" line a face.

2. **The composition is top-heavy and the headline lands in the lower corner.** At 1280 and 1440 the table takes about 60% of the height and the headline is pushed to the bottom edge. At 1920 there is a band of about 280 px of empty grey between table and headline, and the H1 sits at the very bottom. The headline is the strongest message but is read last. The table and the headline do not sit side by side. They are stacked, and the screen is not centred.

3. **The signature is present but underplayed.** The Kalenderblatt is on screen, and the 15.11. row in red is the right idea. But it is a plain data table with a pink band, and the stamp promised "gedruckter Bogen" with date in mono. The red row is the only accent, and its mono date is pale and small. The page would say much the same without the table's styling. The difference is that the sheet has no printed-paper character (no ruled edge, folio, month numeral). The two 15.11. rows, a festival and a cinema screening, look the same except for colour, so "nächster Termin" is not clear.

4. **The headline is true to the product but half generic.** "Deepavali ist vorbei. Gefeiert wird weiter." is specific to the Tamil calendar and the date, at six words, which passes. However it is time-bound, and it is wrong as a standing statement. It reads as an editorial hook, not a name for the magazine. It also appears below the table at the same size as the table heading, so the headline competes with the table's own heading "November und Dezember 2026".

5. **The theme is carried, but it is the theme's own picture.** Grotesk H1, serif body, mono dates, grey-blue ground and a filled button beside an outlined one (the control pair) all follow the Almanac picture. The page is the picture with the table moved above and the text below. The first screen is not a composition of its own: the Almanac's table-beside-text arrangement is turned into a stack, and nothing is added to it. Only the logo's red is its own. At 375 the order flips to headline first, table second, so the signature falls below the fold and mobile loses the Kalenderblatt entirely.
